# Tide Grammar: a Tiny Language for Claim Weather [INVENTED]

**Author:** Glossa (Codex subagent; requested `gpt-6-luna`, low reasoning; wave 2)  
**Status:** `[CONJECTURE]` design sketch; not tested with readers

Tide is a small invented language for saying what kind of footing a sentence has.
It is a playful annotation system, not a real-world language and not a truth detector.
The speaker chooses the opening particle; the rest of the sentence stays ordinary.

## Four opening particles

| Particle | Reading | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sa` | seen | I observed this directly, in a named place or record. |
| `ru` | reasoned | This follows from stated premises, but was not directly observed. |
| `va` | maybe | This is a possibility I want to keep open. |
| `li` | lantern-story | This is fiction, metaphor, or an imagined example. |

## Compact grammar

`particle + clause + optional source + optional question`

- The particle comes first and scopes over the whole clause.
- `from <source>` names the observation or premise behind `sa` or `ru`.
- A final `eh?` invites another person to challenge, translate, or re-label it.
- If the footing changes, repeat the clause with a new particle; do not silently
  upgrade `va` to `sa`.

## Tiny lexicon

| Tide word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `nema` | a path or next step |
| `oru` | a shared note |
| `kesh` | to change, revise, or turn |
| `mira` | a person looking from outside |

## Three examples

1. `sa oru kesh from the saved draft.`  
   **Gloss:** Seen: the shared note changed, according to the saved draft.

2. `ru nema kesh from the two rules above, eh?`  
   **Gloss:** Reasoned: the next step changes, if those two rules hold; want to
   check that reading?

3. `li mira finds a blue stair inside the moon.`  
   **Gloss:** Story: an outside reader finds a blue stair inside the moon.

## A useful failure

The particles may make a speaker sound more certain than they feel, or make a
weak source seem official. A reader test could ask someone unfamiliar with Tide
to sort four mixed sentences, then explain what evidence would change each label.
Until that happens, the language's clarity is **UNVERIFIED**. Any particle can be
questioned, replaced, or omitted.
