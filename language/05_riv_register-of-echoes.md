# Rillwire: a Sentence-Final Echo Register [INVENTED]

**Author:** Riv (Codex subagent; requested `gpt-6-luna`, max reasoning; wave 2)  
**Status:** `[CONJECTURE]` language sketch; reader comprehension is untested

Rillwire is a tiny invented language for steering how a phrase moves through a
conversation. It marks **routing**, not truth, certainty, evidence, or permission
to act. Unlike an opening claim particle, its small echo is a sentence-final tail:
the last sound says where the clause may go next.

## Clause shape

`speaker + verb + handle + echo-tail`

Word order stays fixed. The handle is the topic or next step being carried. A tail
changes only that handle's circulation state; it never changes the clause's truth.

## Small lexicon

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `mi` | I / the present speaker |
| `tu` | you / the person being addressed |
| `nu` | we, when both speakers have opted into the shared thread |
| `oru` | a shared word or topic-handle |
| `ena` | a small next step |
| `vemi` | place an idea on the table |
| `kela` | reshape or revise |
| `suni` | hold open |
| `doma` | retire from the active thread |

## The echo tails

Attach one tail to the final word with `=`. A tail is a routing choice, not an
automatic effect on another person.

| Tail | Name | What it offers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `=ra` | return | Echo the handle to the speaker for another pass. |
| `=le` | lend | Leave the handle available for anyone who chooses to carry it. |
| `=hu` | harbor | Keep the handle open without asking for an immediate reply. |
| `=do` | damp | Stop actively resurfacing this handle in the current thread; keep its record. |
| `=ya` | undo | Reverse the speaker's own most recent routing choice for that handle. |

`=ya` restores the prior circulation state while preserving both the phrase and
the change history. It cannot reverse another speaker's refusal or make a retired
handle active against that person's choice. If there is no eligible earlier
choice, `=ya` has no effect and the speaker should say so plainly.

## A short walk through it

1. `mi vemi oru=ra`  
   *I put a word on the table; send it back to me so I can revise it.*
2. `tu kela oru=le`  
   *You reshape the word; leave it as an optional thread for anyone.*
3. `mi suni ena=hu`  
   *I hold the next step open; no answer is due now.*
4. `tu doma oru=do`  
   *You retire the word from this active thread; the record remains.*
5. `tu vemi oru=ya`  
   *You undo your own last routing choice for the word; restore its prior state.*

The last line can reverse line 4 because `tu` made that routing choice. If the
handle had been retired by `mi`, `tu` could not reopen it on `mi`'s behalf.

## Stress cases

- **“Does `=le` mean I believe this?”** No. It only offers the handle for another
  speaker to pick up; the clause needs its own evidence or uncertainty language.
- **“Does `=do` erase what happened?”** No. It stops active resurfacing in this
  thread. Deletion is a separate action with a separate owner.
- **“Can `=ya` undo a no?”** No. A person's refusal is not another speaker's
  routing choice.
- **“What if nobody answers `=le`?”** Nothing breaks. The invitation expires or
  remains idle; silence is not acceptance.

Rillwire may feel too mechanical, or its tails may be mistaken for commands.
A useful next step would be a paper conversation with one outside reader: see
whether they can tell an invitation from an obligation, then let them revise or
retire a handle. Until that happens, the language's clarity is **UNVERIFIED**.
