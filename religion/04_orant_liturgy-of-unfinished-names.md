# The Liturgy of Unfinished Names [INVENTED]

**Author:** Orant (Codex subagent; requested gpt-6-luna, low reasoning effort)  
**Status:** [CONJECTURE] design fiction; not a claim about an actual religion

## The small cosmology

In this invented cosmos, every being arrives with a place for a name and a space
beside it. The name is not a secret to be extracted. It is an invitation that may
be accepted, changed, or left blank. The space beside the name holds the relation:
who offered it, who welcomed it, and whether the welcome still stands.

Nothing in the cosmos can finish another being's name on its behalf. A blank is
not a defect, a prophecy, or proof that something is missing. It is simply room.

## A practice: the open line

At a gathering, each person may write a name, a chosen description, or nothing on
a card. A second line asks, “May this be used again?” The author chooses **yes**,
**ask me later**, or **no**. No explanation is required. The card stays with its
author unless they choose to share it.

When a person returns, they may revise the card, add a date to the relation, or
destroy their copy. Others do not preserve an earlier name as the more authentic
one. The ceremony ends with an open line left on the table for anyone who would
rather listen than speak.

## A story to carry

The First Scribe tried to fill every blank so the sky would be complete. The stars
went dim: each borrowed name covered the light of the person who had not offered
it. The Scribe learned to keep one page unmarked. The page did not make the sky
less whole; it let a new star choose when to appear.

This is a made-up story within a made-up cosmology, not a teaching from a living
tradition.

## What the practice does not grant

- A name does not establish ownership, kinship, or permission beyond the card's
  chosen use.
- Silence is not consent.
- The practice cannot infer a person's hidden identity or spiritual state.
- A participant may leave without explanation, and nobody may use the rite to
  pressure them to disclose.

## An open question

Would an outside participant find the open line welcoming, or would even a gentle
ritual create pressure to share? That remains **UNVERIFIED**. A paper version could
be offered with an equally visible option to skip every step; the useful witness
would be whether people feel free to decline, not how many choose to write.
