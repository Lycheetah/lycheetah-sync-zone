Standard Ito calculus fails to capture the sensitivity of an agent's terminal conclusion $F(W)$ to an perturbation that occurred at an earlier turn $s < t$. Ito calculus only looks backward in time; it cannot compute functional gradients along the entire random trajectory.

To measure and govern this volatility, we introduce **Malliavin Calculus** (the stochastic calculus of variations).

Let $\mathcal{H} = L^2([0, T])$ be the Cameron-Martin Hilbert space of square-integrable trajectories. For a smooth functional $F = f(W(h_1), \dots, W(h_n))$, the **Malliavin Derivative** $D_t F$ is the stochastic process:

$$D_t F = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(W(h_1), \dots, W(h_n)) h_i(t)$$

$$F = \mathbb{E}[F] + \int_0^T \mathbb{E}[D_t F \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \, dW_t$$

*where $\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(W_s : 0 \le s \le t)$ is the filtration generated by the conversation history up to time $t$.*

*Proof.*
By the Itô representation theorem, every square-integrable martingale can be written as $F = \mathbb{E}[F] + \int_0^T u_t dW_t$ for some adapted process $u_t$. Applying the Malliavin integration-by-parts duality:

$$\mathbb{E}[F \cdot \delta(u)] = \mathbb{E}[\langle DF, u \rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]$$

where $\delta$ is the Skorokhod integral (the adjoint of $D$). Taking conditional expectations on both sides yields $u_t = \mathbb{E}[D_t F \mid \mathcal{F}_t]$ almost surely. $\blacksquare$

def simulate_malliavin_trajectory(N_steps: int = 100, dt: float = 0.01, seed: int = 42) -> dict:

    W = np.cumsum(dW)
    t = np.linspace(0, N_steps * dt, N_steps)

    T = t[-1]
    F_terminal = np.exp(W[-1] - 0.5 * T)

    clark_ocone_integrand = np.exp(W - 0.5 * t)
