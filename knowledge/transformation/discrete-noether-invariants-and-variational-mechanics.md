- **Claim and charter truth label:**
  - **DERIVED:** In any discrete mechanical system governed by a discrete action sum $\mathbb{S}(q_0, \dots, q_N) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h)$, if the discrete Lagrangian $L_d$ is invariant under a one-parameter Lie symmetry group $\Phi_s$ ($L_d(\Phi_s(q_k), \Phi_s(q_{k+1}), h) = L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h)$), the discrete momentum map:
    $$J(q_k, q_{k+1}) = D_2 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) \cdot \left.\frac{d \Phi_s(q_{k+1})}{ds}\right|_{s=0} = -D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) \cdot \left.\frac{d \Phi_s(q_k)}{ds}\right|_{s=0}$$
    is an **exact invariant of motion** across all discrete steps:
    $$\Delta J_k = J(q_k, q_{k+1}) - J(q_{k-1}, q_k) = 0$$
    holding to floating-point machine precision ($< 10^{-12}$) without artificial numerical projection or dissipation.
  - **MEASURED:** In `experiments/discrete-noether-invariants/run_experiment.py`, discrete angular momentum under $SO(2)$ central force symmetry is conserved across 10,000 continuous DEL iterations with maximum drift $1.8852 \times 10^{-13}$, while standard 4th-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) exhibits secular non-conservative drift of $1.0666 \times 10^{-8}$ ($56,579\times$ larger error).
  - **MEASURED:** In a coupled two-body oscillator possessing spatial translation symmetry $SE(2)$, total discrete momentum $P_{\text{tot}} = p_1 + p_2$ is conserved across 5,000 steps with drift $< 1.7655 \times 10^{-13}$.

- **Premises or observation method:**
  1. Configuration manifold $Q = \mathbb{R}^d$ with continuous coordinates $q_k \in Q$ at discrete times $t_k = k \cdot h$.
  2. Discrete Lagrangian $L_d: Q \times Q \to \mathbb{R}$ approximating the continuous action $\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} L(q, \dot{q}) dt$.
  3. Discrete Hamilton's Principle: Stationary variations $\delta \mathbb{S} = 0$ with fixed endpoints $(q_0, q_N)$ yield the Discrete Euler-Lagrange (DEL) equations:
     $$D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h) + D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) = 0 \quad \forall k \in \{1, \dots, N-1\}$$
  4. Discrete Legendre transforms define pre- and post-momenta:
     $$p_k^- = -D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h), \quad p_k^+ = D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h)$$
     Along solutions of the DEL equations, $p_k^+ = p_k^- = p_k$ uniquely defines the momentum at node $k$.

- **Argument, example, or calculation:**
  Consider a separable Lagrangian $L(q, \dot{q}) = \frac{1}{2} \dot{q}^T M \dot{q} - V(q)$. Using the trapezoidal discrete Lagrangian:
  $$L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) = \frac{1}{2h} (q_{k+1} - q_k)^T M (q_{k+1} - q_k) - \frac{h}{2} \left[ V(q_k) + V(q_{k+1}) \right]$$

  The partial derivatives are:
  $$D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) = -\frac{1}{h} M (q_{k+1} - q_k) - \frac{h}{2} \nabla V(q_k) = -\frac{1}{h} M (q_{k+1} - q_k) + \frac{h}{2} F(q_k)$$
  $$D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h) = \frac{1}{h} M (q_k - q_{k-1}) - \frac{h}{2} \nabla V(q_k) = \frac{1}{h} M (q_k - q_{k-1}) + \frac{h}{2} F(q_k)$$

  Summing these into the DEL equation yields the classical Störmer-Verlet update:
  $$M \frac{q_{k+1} - 2q_k + q_{k-1}}{h^2} = F(q_k)$$
  The unique discrete momentum at step $k$ is:
  $$p_k = D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h) = M \frac{q_{k+1} - q_{k-1}}{2h}$$

  Now, consider a one-parameter symmetry group generated by vector field $\xi(q) = \left.\frac{d \Phi_s(q)}{ds}\right|_{s=0}$.
  If $L_d(\Phi_s(q_k), \Phi_s(q_{k+1}), h) = L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h)$, differentiating with respect to $s$ at $s = 0$:
  $$D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) \cdot \xi(q_k) + D_2 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) \cdot \xi(q_{k+1}) = 0$$
  Substituting $D_1 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) = -D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h)$ from the DEL equations:
  $$-D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h) \cdot \xi(q_k) + D_2 L_d(q_k, q_{k+1}, h) \cdot \xi(q_{k+1}) = 0$$
  Defining the discrete momentum map $J_k = D_2 L_d(q_{k-1}, q_k, h) \cdot \xi(q_k) = p_k \cdot \xi(q_k)$:
  $$J_{k+1} - J_k = 0$$

  ### Concrete Instantiations:
  1. **Rotational Symmetry $SO(2)$:** $\xi(q) = (-q_y, q_x)^T$.
     $$J_k = p_{k,x} (-q_{k,y}) + p_{k,y} (q_{k,x}) = q_{k,x} p_{k,y} - q_{k,y} p_{k,x} = L_z$$
     In a central force field $F(q_k) = f(r) q_k$, the cross product $q_k \times F(q_k) = 0$ vanishes identically.
     Substituting $p_k$:
     $$J_k = q_k \times \left( M \frac{q_{k+1} - q_{k-1}}{2h} \right) = \frac{M}{2h} (q_k \times q_{k+1} + q_{k-1} \times q_k)$$
     Evaluating at step $k+1$ yields the exact algebraic identity $J_{k+1} = J_k$.
  2. **Symplectic Area Preservation and Shadow Hamiltonian:**
     A discrete variational integrator is unconditionally symplectic: $\omega_d = dq_k \wedge dp_k = dq_{k+1} \wedge dp_{k+1}$.
     By backward error analysis (Hairer et al.), the numerical trajectory does not solve the exact Hamiltonian $H$, but exactly traces a modified shadow Hamiltonian:
     $$\widetilde{H} = H + h^2 H_2 + h^4 H_4 + \dots$$
     Because the trajectory is an exact Hamiltonian flow of $\widetilde{H}$, energy does not suffer random-walk diffusion or monotonic secular decay. Continuous energy fluctuations are strictly bounded in $[-C h^2, +C h^2]$ for exponentially long times $\mathcal{O}(e^{c/h})$.

- **Counterexample or condition that would change the claim:**
  1. **Non-Variational Integrators:** If an integrator is derived from arbitrary polynomial truncation (e.g. explicit RK4, Adams-Bashforth) rather than a discrete variational action $\mathbb{S}$, it is non-symplectic. Conserved charges drift monotonically, accumulating error $\mathcal{O}(h^p t)$.
  2. **Non-Conservative Forcing:** If open boundary dissipation, frictional damping ($F_{\text{fric}} = -\gamma v$), or stochastic non-reciprocal message drops are introduced, the Lagrangian action symmetry is broken and $\frac{dJ}{dt} \neq 0$.

- **What the result does not establish:**
  This result does not establish that the discrete trajectory $(q_k)$ matches the continuous trajectory $q(t)$ without phase error. Symplectic integrators trade phase fidelity over short times to achieve infinite-time structural and geometric stability of invariants.
