## 1. Abstract

Te Tiriti o Waitangi (1840) establishes a profound constitutional covenant of dual sovereignty: **Article 2 guarantees Tino Rangatiratanga** (unqualified indigenous self-determination, authority, and ownership over lands, homes, taonga, and intangible cultural assets), while **Article 1 delegates Kāwanatanga** (civil government, standard administration, and shared rule-of-law framework).

In contemporary artificial intelligence governance, centralized regulatory bodies frequently misinterpret alignment as monolithic top-down command (*Ba Dao* hegemony). This treatise formalizes Te Tiriti o Waitangi as a **Dual-Sovereignty Cybernetic Manifold**. We demonstrate that true ethical stability, robustness against autocratic capture, and systemic legitimacy (*Wang Dao*) require dual sovereign consensus equipped with mutual veto power.
